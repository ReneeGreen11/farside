**L. plantarum Gelatin Coconut Yogurt**

Ingredients (for 1 can / around 400 ml)

• 1 can (400 ml) additive-free full-fat coconut milk

• 1 capsule Jarrow L. plantarum (emptied)

• 1 tbsp sugar (bacterial food)

• 1 tsp inulin (prebiotic)

• 1 tsp unflavoured gelatin powder (for a spoonable yogurt; use if you prefer it very thick)

Method

1. Bloom & Heat: Pour the cold coconut milk into a small saucepan. Sprinkle the gelatin powder evenly over the top. Let it sit for 3–5 minutes to “bloom” (soften). Turn on medium-low heat and stir constantly until the gelatin is completely dissolved and the liquid is smooth. Do not let it boil.

2. Cool: Pour the milk into your clean glass incubation jar. Let it cool until it reaches 35 to 37 degrees c or 95 to 99 degrees f. It should feel warm to the inner wrist, not hot. (If you add the probiotics while it is too hot, you will kill the bacteria).

3. Inoculate: Stir in the sugar and inulin until dissolved. Empty the Jarrow L. plantarum capsule into the milk and whisk thoroughly.

4. Incubate: Cover the jar loosely. Incubate at 35 to 37 degrees c ( 95 to 99 degrees f) for 18-24 hours. 20 hours is working for me. It will smell clean and pleasantly sour when done. (Note: The mixture will still look quite liquid at this stage; this is normal!)

5. Set in Fridge: Place the jar into the refrigerator for at least 6 to 8 hours. The gelatin and coconut fats will fully solidify and thicken into a creamy, spoonable yogurt during this chill time. Stir gently before serving.

Notes:

The Doubled Ingredient List (for 2 cans / 800 ml)

2 cans (800 ml) additive-free full-fat coconut milk

1 capsule Jarrow L. plantarum (do not double)

2 tbsp sugar (must double; bacteria need twice as much food)

2 tsp inulin (double)

2 tsp unflavoured gelatin powder (double; use 3 tsp if you want it very thick)

If using Coconut milk with guar gum slightly Less Gelatin Needed: Because guar gum is already acting as a thickener, you can safely use 1.5 to 2 teaspoons of gelatin for your 2-can batch. If you use a full, heavy amount of gelatin on top of the guar gum, your yogurt might end up with a slightly bouncy, “Jell-O-like” firmness rather than a creamy spoonable scoop.

To start your next batch, save exactly 2 tablespoons (30 mL) of your finished coconut yogurt.

For your next 2-can batch, you will use these 2 tablespoons instead of a new Jarrow capsule.

How to use your starter yogurt in the next batch:

Keep it fresh: Take out the 2 tablespoons of yogurt within the first few days of making your current batch. Put it in a small, clean, airtight container in the fridge so the bacteria stay strong and active.

Warm it up slightly: When you are ready to make the next batch, take your saved starter yogurt out of the fridge and let it sit on the counter for 15–20 minutes to take the chill off.

Incorporate smoothly: Once your new coconut milk and gelatin mixture has been heated and cooled down below (35-37 95-99), add your 2 tablespoons of sugar and 2 teaspoons of inulin. Then, add your 2 tablespoons of saved yogurt. Whisk vigorously until the starter yogurt is completely dissolved and smoothly blended into the fresh milk before pouring it into your jars.

Ferment slightly faster: Because live, active yogurt cultures wake up much faster than freeze-dried capsule powder, your second batch might ferment quicker. Check it around the 16 to 18-hour mark—if it smells perfectly tangy and sour, you can stop the yogurt maker early!

You can usually repeat this process (using yogurt from the previous batch) about 3 to 4 times. Eventually, wild bacteria from the kitchen air will naturally weaken the L. plantarum strain, and the yogurt might start losing its tang or separating poorly. When that happens, simply start fresh with a brand new Jarrow capsule!
